Q1. The planets nearest to Sun are
(a) Mercury and Mars
(b) Earth and Mercury
(c) Mercury and Venus
(d) Mercury and Uranus
The correct option is (c).
• The order of the planets in our solar system,
starting from the one closest to the Sun, is:
Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn,
Uranus, and Neptune.
• Therefore, the two planets nearest to the Sun
are Mercury and Venus.
• Hence, option (c) is correct.

Q3. You are asked to import ostrich, platypus and
koala bear. Which country would you select to go
where you get all these three?
(a) Japan
(b) Australia
(c) New Zealand
(d) Canada
The correct option is (b).
• The platypus and koala are both endemic to
Australia, meaning they are found only in
Australia.
• While the ostrich is native to Africa, Australia

introduced earlier, and they are also farmed
extensively.

among the options where one could find all
three animals.

Q4. Richter scale is used to measure
(a) Earthquakes
(b) Ocean depth
(c) Intensity of wind
(d) Temperature of the body
The correct option is (a).
• The Richter scale is a logarithmic scale used
to measure the magnitude (energy released)
during an earthquake.
• It measures the amplitude of seismic waves
recorded by seismographs.
• Each whole number increase on the scale
represents a tenfold increase in amplitude
and ~31.6 times more energy release.
• Hence, option (a) is correct.

Q1. What is a continental shelf ?
(a) It is a part of the ocean which is really an extension
of the land mass, but submerged
(b) It is that part where the ocean commences
(c) It is a land mass which is surrounded by water on
all sides
(d) It is a part of the continent that is submerged in

The correct option is (d).
• A continental shelf is the edge of a continent
that lies under the ocean.
• It is a gently sloping, submerged plain that
extends from the coastline to the shelf break,
where the ocean floor drops steeply.
• It is geologically part of the continental crust,
submerged in relatively shallow sea.
• Hence, option (d) is correct.

Q3. International date line
(a) Roughly corresponds to 180th meridian, the
regions to the east of which are counted as being one
day earlier in their calendar dates than regions to the
West
(b) Roughly corresponds to 180th meridian, the region
to the west of which are counted as being one day
earlier in their calendar dates than regions to the east
(c) Roughly corresponds to 90th meridian which falls
on the opposite side of the Greenwich meridian
(d) Roughly corresponds to 135th meridian which falls
on the opposite side of the Greenwich meridian
The correct option is (a).
• The International Date Line (IDL) is an
imaginary line running from the North Pole to
the South Pole.
• It roughly follows the 180° meridian of
longitude through the Pacific Ocean.
• Crossing eastward: subtract a day → regions
east are one day earlier than those west.
• Crossing westward: add a day.
• Hence, option (a) is correct.
Q4. Doldrums refer to
(a) A belt of calm and light variable winds near the
equator
(b) A particular area in the centre of the Pacific Ocean
(c) The region of the upper atmosphere extending
upward from the tropopause to about 20 kms. above
the earth
(d) None of these
The correct option is (a).
• The doldrums is a nautical term for the
Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ).
• It is a low-pressure belt near the equator,
where trade winds converge.
• This leads to calm or light, variable winds and
frequent thunderstorms.
• Historically, this lack of steady wind trapped
sailing ships.
• Hence, option (a) is correct.

(a) Igneous

The correct option is (c).

Q7. When it is 8 A.M. on Wednesday of Greenwich
(a) It is 10.30 P.M. on Wednesday at London
(b) It is 6.15 A.M. on Tuesday at New York
(c) It is 3.00 P.M. on Wednesday at Hong Kong
(d) It is 5.00 P.M. on Wednesday at Tokyo

The correct option is (d).
• Greenwich Mean Time (GMT) is UTC+0.
• Tokyo is at UTC+9, i.e., 9 hours ahead of
GMT.
• So, 8:00 A.M. GMT + 9 hours = 5:00 P.M.
(17:00) Tokyo time.
• Hence, option (d) is correct.
Q8. Heavy rainfall affects soil by
(a) Increasing its acidity
(b) Increasing its alkalinity
(c) Reducing its fertility
(d) None of these

minerals like calcium, magnesium, and
potassium are washed away.
• This leaves behind acidic elements like
aluminium and hydrogen ions, increasing soil
acidity.
• Hence, option (a) is correct.

Q1. It takes 1.3 seconds for the light to reach the
earth from moon and 8 minutes from the sun. How
much time will it take from the nearest bright star to
reach the earth?
(a) 4.5 minutes
(b) 45 minutes
(c) 4.3 light years
(d) 4.3 light years
The correct option is (d).
• The question asks for the time it takes for
light to travel from the nearest bright star to
Earth.
• The nearest star system to our Sun is Alpha
Centauri.
• The brightest star in that system, and one of
the nearest, is Proxima Centauri.
• The distance to Proxima Centauri is
approximately 4.3 light-years.
• A light-year is the distance light travels in one
year. Therefore, by definition, it takes 4.3
years for light from this star to reach us.
• The unit itself represents the travel time.
• Hence, option (d) is correct.
Q2. U.S.A. found rings around which planet?
(a) Venus
(b) Jupiter

While Saturn’s rings have been known for

The rings of Jupiter were discovered by the

These rings are much fainter and smaller than

The question refers to this historic discovery

Q3. When a person moves from poles to the equator,
the population of plants and animals will
(a) Increase
(b) Decrease
(c) Remain unchanged
(d) Not show any consistent behavior
The correct option is (a).
• There is a well-established ecological pattern
known as the latitudinal diversity gradient.
• This pattern shows that biodiversity (the
variety and population of plants and animals)
is lowest at the poles and increases as one
moves towards the equator.
• The equatorial regions, with their stable,
warm, and wet climates, support a much
greater abundance and diversity of life than
the harsh, cold polar regions.
• Hence, option (a) is correct.
Q4. Why U.S.S.R. has many local time differences?
(a) Because of long east-west stretches
(b) Because north-south stretch is more
(c) Because it covers two continents
(d) Because of large differences in climate
The correct option is (a).
• Time zones are based on longitude.
• A country’s local time differences depend on
how many degrees of longitude it spans from
east to west.
• The U.S.S.R. (and its successor, Russia) is the
world’s largest country by area and has an
extremely long east-west stretch, spanning
across 11 time zones.

•

•

This vast longitudinal expanse means that
when it is morning on its western border, it is
already evening on its eastern border,
necessitating the use of multiple time zones.
Hence, option (a) is correct.

Q5. Earthquakes are caused due to
(a) Volcanic eruptions
(b) Landslides
(c) Cyclones
(d) Movement of a part of earth’s surface on account
of the faulting of rocks
The correct option is (d).
• The vast majority of earthquakes are tectonic
in origin.
• They are caused by the sudden release of
energy from the movement of tectonic plates
along fractures in the Earth’s crust called
faults.
• When the stress built up from the plates
pushing against each other exceeds the
strength of the rocks, the rocks break and
slip, causing the ground to shake.
• This process is known as the faulting of rocks.
• While volcanic eruptions and large landslides
can cause minor, localized tremors, they are
not the primary cause of major earthquakes.
• Hence, option (d) is correct.
Q6. Forests help in
(a) Soil erosion
(b) Soil protection
(c) Depleting the soil of its moisture

(d) All of the above
The correct option is (b).
• Forests play a crucial role in soil protection.
• The canopy of trees intercepts rainfall,
reducing the force with which it hits the
ground (preventing splash erosion).
• The network of tree roots binds the soil
particles together, making the soil resistant to
being washed or blown away.
• The layer of leaf litter on the forest floor also
protects the soil and helps it retain moisture.
• Therefore, forests prevent soil erosion and
help in soil protection.
• Hence, option (b) is correct.
Q7. Different seasons are formed because
(a) Sun is moving around the earth
(b) Of revolution of the earth around the sun on its
orbit
(c) Of rotation of the earth around its axis
(d) All of the above
The correct option is (b).
• The primary cause of the seasons is the tilt of
the Earth’s axis (about 23.5 degrees)
combined with its revolution around the Sun.
• As the Earth orbits the Sun, this constant tilt
means that for part of the year, the Northern
Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun
(summer) while the Southern Hemisphere is
tilted away (winter), and vice versa.
• The rotation of the Earth on its axis causes
day and night, not the seasons.
• Hence, option (b) is correct.

Q8. There are two places P and Q. The longitudes of
P and Q are 45° E and 60°W respectively. What will
be the difference in their local times?
(a) 7 hours
(b) 6 hours
(c) 3.5 hours
(d) Both places will have the same local time
The correct option is (a).
• To find the difference in local times, we first
need to find the total difference in longitude.
• Since one place is East and the other is West
of the Prime Meridian, we add their
longitudes: 45° + 60° = 105°.
• The Earth rotates 15° in 1 hour (360° / 24
hours = 15°/hour).
• To find the time difference in hours, we
divide the longitudinal difference by 15: 105°
/ 15° per hour = 7 hours.
• Hence, option (a) is correct.

(d) Does not show any consistent behaviour with the
change in atmospheric temperature
The correct option is (a).
• This question refers to the air’s capacity to
hold moisture, which is known as relative
humidity.
• Warmer air can hold more water vapor than
colder air.
• Therefore, if a source of moisture is available
(like an ocean or wet ground), the amount of
water vapor in the air (absolute humidity)
will increase as the atmospheric temperature
increases.
• This relationship is fundamental to
understanding weather patterns, cloud
formation, and the feeling of “muggy” heat
on a hot, humid day.
• Hence, option (a) is correct.

Q2. The purest form of water is obtained from
(a) A deep tube well
(b) A running stream

(c) Hot water spring
(d) Heavy rains
The correct option is (d).
• The purest form of natural water is
rainwater.
• Water from oceans and other surface bodies
evaporates, leaving behind salts, minerals,
and other impurities.
• This water vapor then condenses to form
clouds and falls as rain.
• While the initial showers might pick up
atmospheric pollutants, water from a
prolonged, heavy rain is exceptionally pure as

streams contains dissolved minerals and
contaminants from the ground.

Q3. Generally speaking, all rocks may be classified
into three major groups, such as
(a) Marble, limestone, igneous
(b) Igneous, limestone, mica
(c) Igneous, marble, sedimentary
(d) Igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic
The correct option is (d).
• Geologists classify all rocks on Earth into
three fundamental groups based on their
process of formation.
• Igneous rocks are formed from the cooling
and solidification of molten rock (magma or
lava).
• Sedimentary rocks are formed from the
accumulation, compaction, and cementation
of sediments over time.
• Metamorphic rocks are formed when existing
igneous or sedimentary rocks are changed by
intense heat, pressure, or chemical action.
• Hence, option (d) is correct.

landmass is provided by
(a) The deserts in Asia and Africa
(b) The similarity in climate of certain regions of Asia
and Africa

(c) The finding of same type of fossil in both the
regions
(d) All of these
The correct option is (c).
• The theory of continental drift, proposed by
Alfred Wegener, suggests that continents
were once joined together in a
supercontinent called Pangaea.
• One of the most compelling pieces of
evidence for this is the discovery of the same
types of fossils of ancient plants and animals
on continents that are now separated by vast
oceans.
• For example, fossils of the reptile
Lystrosaurus have been found in Africa, India
(which later collided with Asia), and
Antarctica, strongly indicating these
landmasses were once connected.
• While climate and geological similarities
provide supporting evidence, the fossil record
is a cornerstone of the theory.
• Hence, option (c) is correct.
Q5. Contour bunding is used
(a) To stop the winds in sandy deserts
(b) To irrigate desert areas
(c) To prevent erosion in hilly areas
(d) None of the above
The correct option is (c).
• Contour bunding, also known as contour
farming or terracing, is a soil conservation
technique.
• It involves creating earthen banks or “bunds”
along the contours of the land on sloped or
hilly areas.
• These bunds act as barriers that slow down
the flow of water down the slope, reducing
its erosive force and allowing more water to
infiltrate the soil.
• This effectively helps to prevent soil erosion.
• Hence, option (c) is correct.

Q3. Which of the following is not favourable for the
formation of deltas?
(a) Calm winds
(b) Currents and tides
(c) Sheltered coast
(d) No large lakes in the river course
The correct option is (b).
• Deltas are landforms created by the
deposition of sediment carried by a river as it
enters a slower-moving or standing body of
water, like an ocean or lake.
• Strong ocean currents and tides are
unfavorable for delta formation because they
actively transport the deposited sediment
away from the river mouth, preventing it
from accumulating and building up a delta.
• Calm conditions, a sheltered coast, and a
river carrying a heavy sediment load (not
trapped in lakes) are all favorable conditions.
• Hence, option (b) is correct.

Q4. Arrange the following three atmospheric layers
starting from the surface of the earth:
A. Stratosphere
B. Ionosphere

Codes :
(a) A, B, C

Q3. Trade winds are caused by the
(a) Revolution of the earth
(b) Rotation of the earth
(c) In flowing movement of air along the earth’s
surface towards the equator
(d) None of the above
The correct option is (c).
• Trade winds are part of a large-scale
atmospheric circulation pattern called the
Hadley Cell.
• Air at the equator is heated, rises, and moves
towards the poles. It then cools, sinks at
around 30° latitude, and creates a highpressure zone.
• From this high-pressure zone, the air flows
back along the surface towards the lowpressure zone at the equator.
• This in-flowing movement of air along the
earth's surface towards the equator forms the
trade winds.
• The rotation of the Earth (Coriolis effect)
deflects these winds, making them
northeasterly in the Northern Hemisphere
and southeasterly in the Southern
Hemisphere.
• Hence, option (c) is correct.
