Q1. Which of the following throws light on Harappan
Culture?
(a) Archaeological excavations
(b) The script on copper sheets
(c) Rock edicts
(d) All of the above
Answer: (a)
• Harappan culture is primarily understood
through archaeological excavations at sites
like Harappa and Mohenjo Daro.
• The Indus script remains undeciphered, and
no copper sheet inscriptions or rock edicts
have been found from this civilization.
• Excavations revealed urban planning, trade
links with Mesopotamia, and material
culture.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.

Q2. Who is regarded as the greatest law giver of
ancient India?
(a) Megasthanese
(b) Panini
(c) Manu
(d) Kautilya
Answer: (c) Manu
• Manu is considered the first man and
legendary lawgiver in Indian mythology.
• He authored the Manusmriti, a foundational
Sanskrit legal text among Hindu
Dharmashastras.
• Medieval Indian rulers traced their lineage to
Manu—solar and lunar dynasties.

•

Manusmriti was translated into English by Sir
William Jones in 1794 and used by the British
to codify Hindu law.
Hence, option (c) is the correct answer.
Q3. At which place Gautama Buddha delivered his
first sermon?
(a) Sarnath
(b) Lumbini
(c) Bodh Gaya
(d) Vaishali
Answer: (a) Sarnath
• After enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, Buddha
went to Sarnath to teach his five former
companions.
• His first sermon, Dhammacakkhapavattana
Sutta, marked the “Turning of the Wheel of
Dharma.”
• This event also established the Sangha, the
Buddhist monastic community.
• Core teachings: Four Noble Truths and Noble
Eightfold Path.
• Ashoka later commemorated the site with
the Lion Capital, now India’s national
emblem.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.
Q4. Neolithic period of age is not characterised by
(a) Agriculture
(b) Use of copper
(c) Domestication of animals
(d) Fishing
Answer: (b) Use of copper
• The Neolithic period marked the shift to food
production—agriculture and animal
domestication.
• Early crops: wheat and barley; early animals:
dog, goat, sheep.
• People settled in villages and used tools made
of stone, not metal.
• Copper use began only towards the end,
leading to the Chalcolithic phase.
Hence, option (b) is the correct answer.

Q5. Name the God who lost his importance in the
beginning of Christian era?
(a) Indra
(c) Vishnu
(b) Brahma
(d) Mahesh
Answer: (a) Indra
• Indra was the chief deity in the Rigveda—god
of thunder, rain, and war.
• He had around 250 hymns dedicated to him
and led the Vedic pantheon.
• By the beginning of the Christian era, his
prominence declined.
• Devotional cults like Bhagavatism (centered
on Vishnu) gained popularity.
• Ritual sacrifices gave way to personal
devotion and newer traditions.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.

Q7. Upanishads are
(a) Religious books of Hindus
(b) Books dealing with ancient Hindu laws
(c) Books on social behaviour of man
(d) Prayers to God
Answer: (a) Religious books of Hindus
• The Upanishads are philosophical and spiritual
texts composed between 800–500 BCE.
• They explore concepts like Brahman (universal
reality) and Atman (individual soul).
• Focus is on self-realization, liberation
(moksha), and the nature of existence.
• Around 200 exist, but 13 are considered
principal—e.g., Chandogya, Brihadaranyaka,
Kena, Katha.
• India’s national motto “Satyameva Jayate”
comes from the Mundaka Upanishad.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.

Q6. We can know about early Vedic period from
(a) Archaeological excavations
(b) Contemporary cultures
(c) Rigveda
(d) Jatak Katha
Answer: (c) Rigveda
• The Rigveda (c. 1500–1200 BCE) is the oldest
and most authentic source of Early Vedic
history.
• It contains 1,028 hymns across 10 mandalas;
Books II–VII are the oldest and reflect early
Vedic life.
• It provides insights into Aryan society,
religion, polity, and economy after the decline
of the Indus Valley Civilization.
• The Gayatri Mantra (3.62.10), dedicated to
Savitr, is a key spiritual verse from Mandala 3.
Hence, option (c) is the correct answer.

Q8. The Indus Valley people had contacts with
(a) Egyptians
(b) Sumerians
(c) Chinese
(d) Mesopotamians
Answer: (d) Mesopotamians
• Mesopotamian texts refer to Meluhha, likely
the Indus Valley region, as a major trading
partner.
• Harappans exported goods like beads, gems,
and cotton via sea routes to Mesopotamian
ports.
• Some Meluhhans even settled in Sumer;
interpreters were appointed for their
language.
• Dilmun (modern Bahrain) acted as a trade
intermediary between the two civilizations.
• No evidence suggests Mesopotamians
reached the Indus—Harappans managed the
trade.
Hence, option (d) is the correct answer.
Q9. In which way Sarnath is associated with Lord
Buddha?
(a) He resided there
(b) He was born there

(c) He ruled there
(d) He preached his first Sermon there
Answer: (d) He preached his first Sermon there
• After attaining enlightenment in Bodhgaya,
Buddha went to Sarnath to meet his five
former companions.
• He delivered his first sermon, known as the
Dhammacakkhapavattana Sutta, marking the
“Turning of the Wheel of Dharma.”
• This event led to the founding of the
Sangha—the Buddhist monastic community.
• His teachings included the Four Noble Truths
and the Noble Eightfold Path.
• Sarnath flourished as a Buddhist center,
especially during the Gupta period (4th–6th
century AD).
• Hsuan Tsang, the Chinese pilgrim, recorded
30 monasteries and 3000 monks in the 7th
century.
• It became a hub for Sammatiya (Hinayana)
and Vajrayana Buddhism, with images of
Heruka and Tara.
Hence, option (d) is the correct answer.

Q10. Lothal is connected as excavation site of the
civilisation of
(a) Indus Valley Civilisation
(b) Sumerians
(c) Mesopotamians
(d) Vedic Aryan
Answer: (a) Indus Valley Civilisation
• Lothal, located in Gujarat, was a major city of
the Indus Valley Civilization, dating to around
2400 BCE.

•

Excavated by S.R. Rao (ASI) between 1955–
1960, it revealed advanced urban planning
and maritime trade.
• It had the world’s earliest known dockyard,
linked to the Sabarmati River, facilitating
trade with West Asia and Africa.
• Artifacts include beads, gems, and
ornaments, indicating a thriving commercial
hub.
• The site was protected by a massive brick
wall, likely for flood control.
• The name Lothal means “mound of the
dead,” similar to Mohenjo-daro in Sindhi.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.

Q11. Which of the following is not related to the
Gandhara School of Art?
(a) Ellora
(b) Ajanta
(c) Khajuraho
(d) Elephanta
Answer: (c) Khajuraho
• The Gandhara School of Art flourished
between 1st century BCE and 5th century CE,
blending Greco-Roman and Indian styles,
mainly in Buddhist sculptures.
• Sites like Ajanta, Ellora, and Elephanta show
Buddhist influence and stylistic connections to
Gandhara.
• Khajuraho, however, is known for its distinct
temple architecture and erotic sculptures,
developed under the Chandella dynasty (950–
1050 CE).

•

Temples at Khajuraho are Hindu and Jain,
with no link to Gandhara’s Buddhist art
tradition.
Hence, option (c) is the correct answer.

Q12. Which of the following is the most important
cause for the decline of Buddhism after Ashoka?
(a) Non-patronage by the kings
(b) Condemnation of animal sacrifices
(c) Growth of licentious practices in Buddhist centres
(d) Allegiance to the middle path
Answer: (a) Non-patronage by the kings
• After rulers like Ashoka, Kanishka, and
Harshavardhan, Buddhism lost significant
royal support.
• Rajput rulers, known for their militant nature,
opposed Buddhism’s non-violent ideals.
• Brahmanical revival under the Guptas further
weakened Buddhist influence.
• Without state patronage, Buddhist
institutions declined and were overshadowed
by Hindu traditions.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.
Q13. Saka era commenced from
(a) 78 AD
(c) 1000 AD
(b) 120 AD
(d) 1953 AD
Answer: (a) 78 AD
• The Saka Era began with the accession of
Kanishka I, the most renowned ruler of the
Kushana dynasty.
• Kanishka was of Central Asian origin,
succeeding Vima Kadphises, who introduced
gold coinage.

•

The Kushanas, also known as Yueh-Chis or
Tocharians, migrated from North Central Asia
into India.
• Kanishka’s reign, starting in 78 AD, marked
the formal start of the Saka calendar, still
used in Indian official contexts.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.
Q14. Which of the following was the most important
characteristic of Kanishka’s rule?
(a) Expansion of Buddhism outside India
(b) Re-emergence of Jainism
(c) Fourth Buddhist Council at Srinagar
(d) Gandhara School of Art
Answer: (c) Fourth Buddhist Council at Srinagar
• Kanishka, the greatest Kushan ruler, presided
over the Fourth Buddhist Council at
Kundalvana (Srinagar) in 72 AD.
• The council focused on Buddhist theology
and doctrine, led by Vasumitra with
Asvaghosa as deputy.
• It marked a turning point in Mahayana
Buddhism and helped systematize Buddhist
texts.
• Though Kanishka also promoted Gandhara art
and Buddhist expansion, the council was his
most defining contribution.
Hence, option (c) is the correct answer.

Q1. Megasthenes visited the court of
(a) Ashoka
(b) Harsha
(c) Chandragupta Maurya
(d) Vikramaditya
Answer: (c) Chandragupta Maurya
• Megasthenes was the Greek ambassador sent
by Seleucus Nicator to the court of
Chandragupta Maurya.
• Their treaty in 305 B.C. marked the
withdrawal of Greek forces and fixed the
Mauryan Empire’s western boundary at the
Hindu Kush.

•

Megasthenes authored Indica, a detailed
account of Indian society, administration, and
urban life.
• He praised Pataliputra, comparing its
grandeur to Persepolis, the Persian capital.
Hence, option (c) is the correct answer.

Q2. Earliest Indian coins belonged to which period?
(a) 5th century B.C.
(b) Ashoka’s reign
(c) 1st century A.D.
(d) 3rd century A.D.
Answer: (c) 1st century A.D.
• The Indo-Greeks were the first to issue coins
with ruler’s name, title, and portrait, starting
in the 1st century A.D.
• These coins reflected high craftsmanship and
supported international trade with West Asia
and the Mediterranean.
• Indigenous coinage evolved, with rulers like
the Satavahanas and Kanishka minting coins
in gold, copper, and alloys.
• Kanishka’s coins featured the king in various
poses and deities on the reverse, showing
artistic and religious diversity.
Hence, option (c) is the correct answer.
UPSC PYQs – 1982
Q1. Out of the following the oldest dynasty is
(a) Mauryas
(b) Nandas
(c) Pallavas
(d) Guptas
Answer: (b) Nandas

•

The Nanda dynasty originated in Magadha
around 345 BCE, making it the earliest among
the listed dynasties.
• They expanded their empire across the Ganga
plains and are considered India’s first empire
builders.
• Dhana Nanda, the last Nanda ruler, was in
power during Alexander’s invasion in 326
BCE.
• The Mauryas succeeded the Nandas; Pallavas
and Guptas emerged much later.
Hence, option (b) is the correct answer.
Q2. The script of Indus Valley civilisation was
(a) Persian
(b) Dravidian
(c) Sanskrit
(d) Undeciphered
Answer: (d) Undeciphered
• The Indus Valley Civilization (2600–1900 BCE)
was South Asia’s earliest urban culture.
• Its script, known as the Harappan script,
remains undeciphered to this day.
• Writing was often boustrophedon—
alternating right-to-left and left-to-right lines.
• Though some scholars suggest a ProtoDravidian link, no consensus exists.
• Inscriptions are short, with around 375–400
unique signs; the longest has just 26
characters.
• Over 4,000 inscribed objects have been
found, including some in Mesopotamia,
indicating trade links.
Hence, option (d) is the correct answer.

Q1. The philosophical systems of the Hindus were
propounded and properly codified in the
(a) Vedic age
(b) Maurya age
(c) Kanishka age
(d) Gupta age
Answer: (a) Vedic age
• Hindu philosophy emerged during the Vedic
age, rooted in the Vedas and later texts like
the Upanishads and Vedangas.
• The six orthodox systems—Samkhya, Yoga,
Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa, and
Vedanta—were developed from Vedic
thought.
• These systems are known as Astika darsanas,
as they accept the Vedas as authoritative.
• The Rigveda, especially its later mandalas, and
auxiliary texts like the Vedangas, laid the
foundation for codifying philosophical ideas.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.
Q2. Which of the following statements is not correct
about Nalanda University?
(a) It was an ancient Buddhist learning centre
(b) A Chinese Hiuen Tsang studied here
(c) We came to know about it only through the
writings of Chinese pilgrims
(d) It was mainly responsible for spreading Buddhism
to China, Japan and South East Asia
Answer: (c) We came to know about it only through
the writings of Chinese pilgrims
• Nalanda University was a renowned Buddhist
learning centre in Magadha (modern Bihar).
• It flourished under the Gupta Empire and
later Harsha of Kannauj.
• Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) studied here and
documented its grandeur.
• However, information about Nalanda comes
from multiple sources, including Indian
inscriptions, archaeological evidence, and
literary texts, not just Chinese pilgrims.
• It was a hub for international students and
contributed to the spread of Buddhism, but

not solely responsible for its expansion to
China, Japan, and Southeast Asia.
Hence, option (c) is the correct answer.

Q3. Ancient Indian art and architecture reached its
zenith during the period of
(a) Rajputs
(b) Mauryas
(c) Guptas
(d) None of these
Answer: (c) Guptas
• The Gupta period (4th–6th century CE)
marked the golden age of Indian art and
architecture.
• It introduced freestanding stone temples,
replacing earlier brick and wood structures.
• Temples like the Dasavatara Temple at
Deogarh and Bhitargaon show advanced
design and ornamentation.
• Sanchi, Udaygiri, and Tigawa reveal early
Gupta shrines and sculptural finesse.
• Sarnath Buddhas and mukhalingas reflect
spiritual depth and artistic perfection.
• Gupta art absorbed elements from Gandhara,
especially in facial stylization.
• Hence, option (c) is the correct answer.

Q4. Which of the following presents the most
significant features of Indus Valley Civilisation?
(a) Buildings with perfect arches
(b) Use of burnt mud bricks
(c) Use of sun dried mud bricks
(d) None of these
Answer: (b) Use of burnt mud bricks
• The Indus Valley Civilization was known for
its advanced town planning, with streets laid
out in a grid pattern.
• Cities like Harappa, Mohenjodaro, and
Kalibangan had citadels and lower towns
built using burnt bricks, not stone or sundried bricks.
• Burnt bricks were used extensively in houses,
walls, and drainage systems, showcasing
engineering skill.
• The civilization lacked perfect arches, and
stone architecture was virtually absent.
Hence, option (b) is the correct answer.

Q5. What was the main feature of Maurya dynasty in
India?
(a) Education was widespread
(b) Terrorists were driven out of Sind and Punjab
(c) Removal of land tax for the first time
(d) Decentralisation of administration
Answer: (d) Decentralisation of administration
• The Maurya dynasty marked the rise of
monarchy, replacing earlier republics and
oligarchies.
• Kautilya’s Arthashastra emphasized a
consultative administration, not royal
absolutism.
• A Mantriparishad (Council of Ministers)
assisted the king—members included
Purohita, Mahamantri, Senapati, and
Yuvaraja.
• Amatyas, akin to modern civil servants,
managed daily governance; their selection
was systematized.
• Ashoka appointed Dhamma Mahamatras to
oversee moral and religious affairs.
• The administration was divided into 18
departments (tirthas), each handling specific
functions like industry, treasury, and security.
Hence, option (d) is the correct answer.

Q7. Main preachings of Buddha were in regard to
(a) Right faith and conduct
(b) Love of God
(c) Practice of rituals
(d) Idol worship
Answer: (a) Right faith and conduct
Core Teachings of Buddha
• Buddha’s philosophy centers on ethical
living, mental discipline, and spiritual insight,
not ritualism or theism.
• His teachings are encapsulated in the Four
Noble Truths:
o Life is full of suffering (dukkha)
o Suffering is caused by desire (tanha)
o Ending desire ends suffering
o The path to end suffering is the
Eightfold Path
The Eightfold Path (Right Conduct Framework)
• Right View
• Right Resolve
• Right Speech
• Right Conduct
• Right Livelihood
• Right Effort
• Right Mindfulness
• Right Concentration
Additional Principles
• Ahimsa (non-violence) and compassion were
central to his moral code
• He emphasized karma and rejected the
existence of a permanent soul

•

Buddha neither affirmed nor denied God’s
existence, focusing instead on self-realization
and ethical living
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer, as Buddha’s
teachings were fundamentally about right faith and
conduct.

Q1. Gautam Buddha attained Nirvana at
(a) Bodh Gaya
(b) Sarnath
(c) Kushi Nagar
(d) Lumbini
Answer: (a) Bodh Gaya
• Nirvana in Buddhist context refers to
Enlightenment, not physical death.
• Gautam Buddha attained Nirvana at Bodh
Gaya, under the Bodhi tree, after intense
meditation at the age of 35.
• This moment transformed him into “The
Buddha”, meaning “The Enlightened One.”
• He later delivered his first sermon at Sarnath,
and passed away at Kushinagar, which is
referred to as Mahaparinirvana (final
liberation).
• Emperor Ashoka, a devout Buddhist,
commemorated Bodh Gaya by establishing
the Mahabodhi Temple and the Vajrasana
(Diamond Throne)—the symbolic Seat of
Enlightenment.
Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.

denoted geography
• The correct term for the religious tradition

Hence, option (a) is the correct answer.

(c) a larger earthen pot driven by bullocks
(d) a large water bucket pulled up by rope directly by
hand
The correct option is (b).
• The term 'Araghatta' refers to a waterwheel
mechanism used for irrigation in medieval
India, often known as the Persian wheel.
• The device consists of a large wheel, often
driven by animal power (like bullocks), with a
series of pots or buckets attached to its rim.
• As the wheel turns, the pots dip into a water
source (like a well), fill with water, are lifted
up, and then empty their contents into a
channel for irrigating fields.
• The name itself is derived from the Sanskrit
words 'ara' (spoke) and 'ghatta' (pot).
• Hence, option (b) is correct.
